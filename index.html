02 The Inheritance

The starting point.
The inheritance we received.

In January 1986 the National Resistance Movement took charge of a state that had been hollowed out by a quarter-century of upheaval. This chapter records that starting point plainly — not to dramatise it, but because the distance travelled cannot be measured without it.

  1. I

    A collapsed economy

    Hyperinflation and a broken currency, after years of conflict and misrule.

    $330 GDP per capita · 1986

  2. II

    Darkness after dusk

    One ageing dam, just sixty megawatts, and firewood meeting almost all energy.

    60 MW national generation · 1986

  3. III

    A cycle of instability

    Decades of coups, dictatorship and civil war — a nation desperate for peace.

    56% living in poverty

From this baseline, the record that follows — every figure verified against the sources ledger — is the measure of forty years.

04 The Journey

A journey of courage.
A record of transformation.

Six eras, one continuous line. The imagery of each era is held inside the shape of the nation itself — scroll, and the decades pass through it.

The Republic · one continuous record

1986

Foundation

The National Resistance Movement takes power in a country exhausted by conflict. The work begins with the hardest material there is: trust.

  • 1986 A fundamental change — the rebuilding of the nation begins.
  • 1987 Currency reform and an IMF-backed recovery programme begin to tame hyperinflation.

1990s

Stabilisation

A new constitution, the first direct presidential election, and a public-health campaign the world would study. The state finds its footing.

  • 1995 A new national constitution resets the legal foundation.
  • 1996 The first directly elected president in Uganda's history takes office.
  • 1997 Universal Primary Education opens the school gates to millions.
  • 1990s National HIV prevalence falls by around seventy percent — a global model.

2000s

Growth

The economy compounds. Farms feed factories; enrolment climbs; a landmark refugee law opens the national door and keeps it open.

  • 2006 A landmark Refugee Act — among the world's most progressive — is enacted.
  • 2007 Vision 2040, the long-term blueprint for a modern nation, is approved.

2010s

Transformation

Power on the Nile. Expressways, bridges and transmission lines carry the decade; the grid reaches districts that had never been connected.

  • 2019 The Isimba Dam and the Kampala–Entebbe Expressway come online.

2020s

Acceleration

Karuma lifts national capacity past two thousand megawatts. Industry deepens, and the first barrels of commercial oil come into view.

  • 2024 The 600 MW Karuma Dam lifts national capacity past 2,000 MW.
  • 2026 Forty years of nation-building — as commercial oil production begins.

Beyond

The Next Horizon

The record does not close; it compounds. The next chapter is capacity — industrial, human, and institutional.

  • 2040 Vision 2040: a transformed Ugandan society — from a peasant to a modern and prosperous country.

05 Achievements

Built on conviction.
Delivered for generations.

A national atlas in six pillars — what changed in daily life, and the evidence for it.

Pillar 01

Economy & Livelihoods

From collapse to one of Africa's steadier growth stories.

The government of 1986 inherited hyperinflation and a broken currency. Reform came in sequence — recovery programme, currency, revenue institutions — and held. Real income per person has roughly tripled, and the economy has diversified far beyond subsistence farming.

Pillar 02

Energy & Infrastructure

The lights came on — and stayed on.

From a single ageing dam generating sixty megawatts, four major hydropower stations on the Nile — led by the 600 MW Karuma — have lifted national capacity past two thousand. Expressways, new Nile bridges and a revived national airline reconnected the country and its neighbours.

Pillar 03

Social Progress

Poverty fell from more than half the nation to one in six.

Against the national line, poverty has fallen from 56 percent to 16. Parish-level programmes, water links, rural electrification and enterprise funds carried the gains beyond the capital, into the villages where most Ugandans live.

Pillar 04

Health, Education & Human Capital

A classroom for every child; a turnaround the world studied.

Uganda was the first country in sub-Saharan Africa to confront HIV openly — prevalence fell by around seventy percent through the 1990s, a decline researchers compared to a highly effective vaccine. Universal Primary Education, from 1997, lifted adult literacy from 43 percent to above 70.

Pillar 05

Regional Responsibility

Africa's most generous open door.

Uganda hosts about 1.8 million refugees — more than any other African country — under the 2006 Refugee Act, widely regarded as among the most progressive in the world. Not camps behind fences: a plot of land, freedom of movement, the right to work. The world calls it the Uganda Model.

09 What’s Next

The next chapter is capacity.

The foundation is laid. The decade ahead turns four decades of groundwork into shared prosperity — hopeful, and stated calmly, the way this record states everything.

  1. 01

    First Oil

    With commercial reserves confirmed at Lake Albert, production begins via a pipeline to the coast — revenue for the build-out of everything below.

    ≈230,000 barrels/day at peak

  2. 02

    An Industrial Economy

    From under a hundred factories to thousands — steel, minerals and manufacturing now exporting across the region. The target is a tenfold economy.

    Vision 2040

  3. 03

    Energy in Surplus

    Clean hydropower beyond domestic demand: the grid becomes an export, and a regional stabiliser.

    2,048 MW and rising

  4. 04

    Youth & Skills

    The youngest workforce on the continent, moving from classroom to workshop to enterprise — the true carrier of the next forty years.

    Median age under 18

  5. 05

    Agriculture, Value-Added

    Coffee, dairy and grain increasingly processed at home — raw produce leaving as finished goods.

    Africa's top coffee exporter

  6. 06

    Technology & Innovation

    A digital public infrastructure and a young builder class connecting government, markets and diaspora.

    The new backbone

  7. 07

    Regional Leadership

    A trade hub, a peacekeeping contributor, and the region's most generous host — leadership by evidence, not declaration.

    East African anchor

  8. 08

    National Resilience

    Institutions, food security and an energy surplus — capacity to absorb shocks that once would have broken the state.

    Built to last

10 The Record Continues

Four decades. One journey.
A transformed nation.

Forty years ago, a nation in ruins. Today, a nation on the rise. The work continues — but the distance travelled is a story worth telling, and worth honouring. This record remains open, to be extended by those who come next.
