02 · The Inheritance

The starting point. The inheritance we received.

In January 1986 the National Resistance Movement took charge of a state hollowed out by a quarter-century of upheaval: a collapsed economy ($330 per person), darkness after dusk (60 MW from one ageing dam), and a cycle of instability that had normalised the coup. “This is not a mere change of guard — it is a fundamental change,” the nation was told at the swearing-in. The forty years since are the measure of that promise.

04 · The Journey

Six eras, one continuous line

1986 Foundation. The National Resistance Movement takes power in a country exhausted by conflict. The work begins with the hardest material there is: trust.
1986 — A fundamental change — the rebuilding of the nation begins. · 1987 — Currency reform and an IMF-backed recovery programme begin to tame hyperinflation.
1990s Stabilisation. A new constitution, the first direct presidential election, and a public-health campaign the world would study. The state finds its footing.
1995 — A new national constitution resets the legal foundation. · 1996 — The first directly elected president in Uganda's history takes office. · 1997 — Universal Primary Education opens the school gates to millions. · 1990s — National HIV prevalence falls by around seventy percent — a global model.
2000s Growth. The economy compounds. Farms feed factories; enrolment climbs; a landmark refugee law opens the national door and keeps it open.
2006 — A landmark Refugee Act — among the world's most progressive — is enacted. · 2007 — Vision 2040, the long-term blueprint for a modern nation, is approved.
2010s Transformation. Power on the Nile. Expressways, bridges and transmission lines carry the decade; the grid reaches districts that had never been connected.
2019 — The Isimba Dam and the Kampala–Entebbe Expressway come online.
2020s Acceleration. Karuma lifts national capacity past two thousand megawatts. Industry deepens, and the first barrels of commercial oil come into view.
2024 — The 600 MW Karuma Dam lifts national capacity past 2,000 MW. · 2026 — Forty years of nation-building — as commercial oil production begins.
Beyond The Next Horizon. The record does not close; it compounds. The next chapter is capacity — industrial, human, and institutional.
2040 — Vision 2040: a transformed Ugandan society — from a peasant to a modern and prosperous country.

05 · Achievements

Built on conviction. Delivered for generations.

01 · Economy & Livelihoods

From collapse to one of Africa's steadier growth stories.

The government of 1986 inherited hyperinflation and a broken currency. Reform came in sequence — recovery programme, currency, revenue institutions — and held. Real income per person has roughly tripled, and the economy has diversified far beyond subsistence farming.

3× real income per person since 1986 · ~6.5% average annual growth across the arc · ~5,000 factories operating, from 80

02 · Energy & Infrastructure

The lights came on — and stayed on.

From a single ageing dam generating sixty megawatts, four major hydropower stations on the Nile — led by the 600 MW Karuma — have lifted national capacity past two thousand. Expressways, new Nile bridges and a revived national airline reconnected the country and its neighbours.

2,048 MW installed capacity, from 60 MW · 600 MW Karuma — the nation's largest plant · 51 km Kampala–Entebbe Expressway

03 · Social Progress

Poverty fell from more than half the nation to one in six.

Against the national line, poverty has fallen from 56 percent to 16. Parish-level programmes, water links, rural electrification and enterprise funds carried the gains beyond the capital, into the villages where most Ugandans live.

16% national poverty rate, from 56% · 51% of households with electricity, from ~12% · 1,200+ new piped-water connections in the central region alone

04 · Health, Education & Human Capital

A classroom for every child; a turnaround the world studied.

Uganda was the first country in sub-Saharan Africa to confront HIV openly — prevalence fell by around seventy percent through the 1990s, a decline researchers compared to a highly effective vaccine. Universal Primary Education, from 1997, lifted adult literacy from 43 percent to above 70.

~70% fall in HIV prevalence through the 1990s · 1997 Universal Primary Education opens the gates · 70%+ adult literacy, from 43%

05 · Regional Responsibility

Africa's most generous open door.

Uganda hosts about 1.8 million refugees — more than any other African country — under the 2006 Refugee Act, widely regarded as among the most progressive in the world. Not camps behind fences: a plot of land, freedom of movement, the right to work. The world calls it the Uganda Model.

1.8M refugees hosted — Africa's largest · 2006 landmark Refugee Act · Bidi Bidi among the world's largest settlements, in Yumbe

06 · Stability & Continuity

The foundation of everything else.

After decades defined by coups and civil war, four decades of constitutional order became the precondition for every other line in this record. Investment, schooling, health and growth all rest on it.

40 years of unbroken stability · 1986 the turning point · 1995 the constitution that holds

06 · The Regions

One country. Many regions. One national record.

Central Region

Kampala · Wakiso · Mukono · Masaka

51 km Kampala–Entebbe Expressway · 1,200+ new water links

The Kampala–Entebbe Expressway — the flagship modern highway. The densest factory belt and industrial parks in the nation. The Parish Development Model rolled out across its districts. Piped-water extensions reaching fast-growing suburbs.

Eastern Region

Jinja · Mbale · Soroti · Tororo

2 dams on the Nile · 50 MW grid solar

Isimba and Bujagali hydropower stations on the Victoria Nile. Soroti and Tororo solar plants feeding the national grid. Grain and produce value-addition mills for cooperatives. Jinja's revival as an industrial and tourism centre.

Northern Region

Gulu · Lira · Arua · Moroto

600 MW Karuma — the nation's largest plant · Bidi Bidi among the world's largest settlements

The 600 MW Karuma Dam — the nation's largest power plant. Recovery and rebuilding across the post-conflict north. West Nile electrification reaching long-isolated districts. Home to Bidi Bidi, among the world's largest refugee settlements.

Western Region

Mbarara · Hoima · Fort Portal · Kabale

230k barrels/day at peak, Lake Albert · Nakivale among Africa's oldest settlements

The Lake Albert oilfields and the nation's energy future. A thriving dairy and agriculture economy. Emyooga and SACCO enterprise support for traders. Gateway to the western tourism circuit — and host to Nakivale.
